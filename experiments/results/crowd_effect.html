Do more ideas give a better winner?

Yes: on 1,874 real stories, picking the best of 4 headline ideas got +20.9% more clicks than using a single idea, measured on readers who weren't used to pick it (95% range +19.7% to +22.1%). If every idea were equally good, the same method gives -0.1%, so the gain comes from real differences between ideas, not luck.

How this was measured

Data: the Upworthy Research Archive, 22,666 headline versions from 4,873 real tests (2013 to 2015). Only versions with the same image, excerpt, lede and share text were compared, so the headline is the only difference; versions seen by fewer than 1,000 people were left out.

Each idea's readers were split at random into two halves. The winner of every possible group of N ideas was chosen by its click rate in one half and scored by its click rate in the other half, so the score comes from readers who played no part in choosing it. This was repeated with 40 different random splits. "1 idea" is the average click rate of a story's ideas. The 95% range comes from 1,000 re-samples of the stories. "If all ideas were equally good" runs the same steps on copies of the data where every idea's clicks are redrawn from its story's overall rate; it should be close to 0%. The last column picks and scores on the same readers, which counts the winner's lucky streak as quality (the "winner's curse"), so it is bigger than the honest number.
